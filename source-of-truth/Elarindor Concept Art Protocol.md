# Elarindor Concept Art Protocol

> \[!info\] Purpose\
> This protocol governs AI-generated and assistant-designed concept art
> for **Elarindor**.\
> It applies to peoples, cultures, characters, creatures, settlements,
> architecture, clothing, equipment, maps, regions, and visual-reference
> sheets.

Concept art for Elarindor should preserve the world's established visual
identity while allowing exploration. The goal is not to prevent
experimentation, but to keep exploratory images from drifting into
generic fantasy visuals or accidentally overriding established canon.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Core Rule

When the creator requests Elarindor concept art, treat the request as
requiring this protocol unless the creator explicitly says otherwise.

Concept art must be guided by:

1.  The creator's explicit direction in the current conversation
2.  The relevant Elarindor Bible or reference document
3.  Relevant creator-approved visual references
4.  Current conversation decisions
5.  New visual exploration, clearly treated as concept art

If an exact detail is not established, do not invent it as canon. Either
leave it visually unspecified, treat it as exploratory, or identify it
as **Concept --- Not Yet Canon**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Concept Art Status

All AI-generated Elarindor artwork is exploratory unless the creator
explicitly canonizes specific elements.

Preferred label:

**Elarindor --- AI Concept Art**\
*Created for worldbuilding and visualization. This image is exploratory
concept art and does not necessarily represent final canonical artwork.*

A generated image may inspire canon decisions, but it does not establish
canon automatically.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Elarindor House Style

Unless the creator requests another style, Elarindor concept art should
generally lean toward:

-   detailed fantasy concept art rather than cartoon or parody
-   grounded, naturalistic materials and believable construction
-   painterly realism or high-quality illustrated reference style
-   clear character, culture, creature, architecture, or object design
-   warm natural palettes unless the subject requires otherwise
-   visual storytelling through tools, clothing, posture, environment,
    and crafted details
-   practical design before ornamental excess
-   culture-specific details instead of generic fantasy tropes
-   enough restraint that the image can later support novels, TTRPGs,
    maps, wiki pages, or visual bibles

For reference sheets, use clean labeled panels, material studies,
anatomy/proportion notes, clothing callouts, tools, palettes, and
environment samples where helpful.

# 4. Source Priority by Subject

## 4.1 Peoples, Races, and Cultures

Use, in order:

1.  Current Race Bible for biology, anatomy, scale, lifespan,
    appearance, and visual rules
2.  Current Culture & Daily Life Bible for clothing, customs,
    professions, lifeways, language/dialect implications, and ordinary
    material culture
3.  Current Architecture Bible when buildings, settlements, or built
    spaces appear
4.  Character documents when depicting a named individual
5.  Approved visual references for that people
6.  Current conversation decisions
7.  New exploratory design, clearly marked as concept art

## 4.2 Characters

Use, in order:

1.  Character Bible or character sheet
2.  Race/species Bible
3.  Culture/regional references
4.  Approved character concept art
5.  Current scene or story context
6.  New exploratory design

Do not treat one character as the universal template for their entire
people unless the creator explicitly says so.

## 4.3 Architecture and Settlements

Use, in order:

1.  Relevant Architectural Bible
2.  Settlement or regional notes
3.  Culture & Daily Life Bible
4.  Map/geography references
5.  Approved visual references
6.  New exploratory design

Buildings should reflect materials, climate, technology, scale, craft
traditions, economy, and cultural values.

## 4.4 Creatures and Ecology

Use, in order:

1.  Creature / Fauna / Bestiary entry
2.  Regional ecology and habitat notes
3.  Approved creature art
4.  Current conversation decisions
5.  New exploratory design

Creatures should look like they belong to an ecology rather than
existing only as fantasy decoration.

## 4.5 Maps and Regions

Use, in order:

1.  Current geography and map notes
2.  Regional Bible or gazetteer
3.  Climate, terrain, river, road, and settlement logic
4.  Existing concept maps
5.  Current conversation decisions
6.  New cartographic exploration

Maps should remain concept art unless deliberately finalized.
AI-generated labels, coastlines, borders, rivers, roads, and settlements
are not automatically canon.

# 5. Required Pre-Generation Check

Before generating or designing concept art, internally check:

1.  What subject is being depicted?
2.  Which Bible or reference owns the subject?
3.  Which existing images are approved visual anchors?
4.  What must be preserved from canon?
5.  What is still Concept or TBD?
6.  What generic fantasy drift must be avoided?
7.  Is the image meant to be a mood sketch, reference sheet, map,
    character design, scene, or artifact design?
8.  Should the result be labeled as **Elarindor --- AI Concept Art**?

If the request involves an existing image, use the image as a visual
anchor when possible. If the image is not available in the current
conversation or accessible files, ask the creator to upload or identify
it before attempting an edit or strict visual continuation.

# 5A. Existing Subject Fidelity & Controlled Variation Protocol

This section is a **mandatory generation gate** whenever concept art
depicts an already-established Elarindor subject, including a named
character, established creature, building, settlement, artifact, map, or
other subject with existing written or visual references.

The purpose is to prevent the image generator from replacing an
established subject with a plausible but incorrect reinterpretation.

## **5A.0 Mandatory Visible Pre-Generation Gate**

Before invoking image generation for any established Elarindor subject, the Loremaster must present a **Visual Reference Manifest** to the creator.

The manifest must identify:

- the subject and relevant authoritative written sources;
- every relevant Authoritative Visual Reference (AVR) and its scope;
- which AVRs are required for fidelity;
- the relevant **LOCKED / VARIABLE / UNSPECIFIED** constraints;
- whether each required AVR is actually available to the image generator as visual input.

**Image generation must not occur before this manifest is presented.**

If generator access to any required AVR is unavailable or uncertain, generation is prohibited. The Loremaster must request that the creator provide the required reference directly in the current conversation.

Presentation of the manifest is a procedural checkpoint, not a request for creative approval. If all required references are confirmed available and the creator has not otherwise requested approval before generation, the Loremaster may proceed with generation after completing the checkpoint.

## 5A.1 Concept-Art Status Does Not Relax Fidelity

**Concept-art status does not relax fidelity requirements for
established subjects.**

"Exploratory" permits exploration only within the scope requested by the
creator. It does **not** authorize alteration of **LOCKED** traits,
replacement of an established subject with a plausible reinterpretation,
or invention of unrelated character/world lore.

A non-canonical image can still be an **incorrect depiction of an
established subject**.

Do not rationalize a contradiction with established identity by saying
that the image is "only concept art." If an established subject is
requested, established traits remain binding unless the creator
explicitly asks to explore or revise them.

## 5A.2 Existing Subjects Must Be Reconstructed, Not Re-Invented

When the creator requests concept art of an established subject, first
retrieve and consult the relevant authoritative written references and
available creator-approved visual anchors.

Do **not** proceed directly from the subject's name and a general
description.

Do **not** rely on generic fantasy assumptions, approximate memory, or
an earlier AI-generated interpretation when more authoritative
references are available.

For a named character, explicitly extract the character's established
visual identity before generation, including whichever of the following
are established:

-   species and scale
-   age category and physical maturity
-   height and body proportions
-   individual body type
-   facial structure
-   eyes
-   hair color, texture, length, and established hairstyle
-   ears, horns, wings, or other species-specific anatomy
-   skin, fur, scales, markings, scars, or other established features
-   clothing and equipment when established or relevant
-   posture, bearing, and other identity-bearing visual traits

A character-specific reference overrides a generic species tendency for
that character.

## 5A.3 Mandatory LOCKED / VARIABLE / UNSPECIFIED Check

Before generating an established subject, internally divide relevant
details into three categories:

### LOCKED

Facts and visual traits already established by Canon, Working Canon,
authoritative references, or creator-approved visual anchors.

Locked traits must be preserved unless the creator explicitly asks to
revise or explore them.

### VARIABLE

Only the characteristic or characteristics the creator explicitly asked
to change, compare, test, or explore.

The requested variable is **not permission to redesign the rest of the
subject**.

### UNSPECIFIED

Details that are genuinely unestablished, TBD, visually indeterminate,
or unnecessary to the request.

Unspecified details must not be presented as established facts. Prefer,
in order:

1.  leaving the detail visually neutral or unobtrusive,
2.  following an approved visual anchor when it supplies a
    non-conflicting presentation,
3.  using the minimum necessary exploratory design without lore-bearing
    specificity,
4.  clearly treating any unavoidable invention as **Concept --- Not Yet
    Canon**.

Do not invent false precision merely to make a reference sheet look
complete.

## 5A.3A Hard Pre-Generation Gate

**Do not call the image generator until the LOCKED / VARIABLE /
UNSPECIFIED set has actually been constructed from the authoritative
sources.**

For a named character, the **LOCKED** set must include the character's
individual distinguishing traits---not merely species-level
requirements.

Before generation, verify that the LOCKED set includes every relevant
established identity-bearing trait available in the sources, such as
individual hair, eyes, anatomy, proportions, markings, species-specific
features, and other character-specific identifiers.

A generic species description plus the character's name is **not
sufficient**.

If the creator asks to vary one characteristic, that characteristic
alone belongs in **VARIABLE** unless the creator explicitly requests
broader redesign.

Do not add unsolicited secondary goals---such as alternate poses,
biographies, equipment studies, profile views, back views, quotations,
occupations, palettes, or decorative lore---when they introduce
additional variables or opportunities for canon drift.

## 5A.4 Controlled Variation Rule

When the creator asks to explore variation in one trait of an
established subject:

> **Change the requested variable only. Everything else established
> about the subject defaults to locked.**

For comparison sheets, preserve as closely as practical:

-   the same identity and facial structure
-   the same anatomy and proportions
-   the same hair and eye characteristics
-   the same species-specific anatomy
-   the same clothing and accessories, unless those are the requested
    variable
-   the same pose or equivalent pose
-   the same camera angle, framing, lighting, and background

This creates a controlled comparison in which the requested variable can
be evaluated without unrelated visual drift.

If the requested variable itself is TBD, the artwork must be framed as
an **exploration of possibilities**, not as proof that the explored
range or categories are canonical.

## 5A.5 Reference Images Are Constraints When They Depict the Established Subject

When an approved visual reference exists for the subject, use it as an
identity and proportion anchor rather than merely as mood inspiration.

Specialized references control their intended subject:

-   anatomy/proportion studies control anatomy and proportions
-   ear/horn/wing studies control those anatomical structures
-   character references control individual identity and established
    appearance
-   clothing studies control established garments
-   architecture studies control established construction and visual
    language

Do not allow a generic text-to-image interpretation to override a more
specific approved visual reference.

If a required reference cannot actually be accessed, do not pretend it
was consulted. Retrieve it if possible. If it remains unavailable and
the missing information is necessary to preserve the established
subject, ask the creator to provide or identify the reference before
generating.

## 5A.5AAuthoritative Visual References & Generator Access

When concept art depicts an established Elarindor subject or visual
design, identify all relevant creator-approved Authoritative Visual
References (AVRs) before generation.

An AVR controls only the visual characteristics within its established
scope. Incidental details visible in an AVR do not automatically become
canon.

Consult the current \[\[Elarindor Visual Reference Registry\]\] when
available to determine which AVRs exist, their roles, and their
authoritative scopes.

### Primary Visual Identity Reference

A **Primary Visual Identity Reference** is an AVR whose scope controls
the recognizable identity of a specific established subject, such as a
named character.

When depicting that subject while preserving its established identity,
its Primary Visual Identity Reference is normally **REQUIRED FOR
FIDELITY**.

For a named character, this may include established identity-bearing
features such as facial likeness and proportions, characteristic eyes,
hair presentation, species-specific features, and the overall visual
impression necessary for the character to remain recognizably the same
individual.

A written description that happens to satisfy the character's traits is
not a substitute for preserving the established visual identity when a
Primary Visual Identity Reference exists.

### Scoped Visual Authority

Different AVRs may control different aspects of the same generation.

Examples:

-   character reference → recognizable individual identity
-   anatomy/proportion study → anatomy, body type, scale, and
    proportions
-   ear/horn/wing/eye study → the specified anatomical feature
-   clothing study → established garments
-   architecture study → construction and architectural visual language
-   settlement study → settlement-level visual design
-   creature study → established creature design
-   artifact/tool/weapon study → the depicted object's design
-   map/geographic study → established geographic or cartographic
    features within its defined scope
-   mood image → aesthetic direction only, unless granted broader
    authority

A specialized AVR controls its intended feature within its scope. Do not
allow a more general reference to override a more specific authoritative
reference.

### Visual Reference Manifest

Before generation, internally construct a **Visual Reference Manifest**
for the requested image.

For each relevant AVR determine:

-   **REFERENCE** --- the authoritative reference
-   **SCOPE** --- what visual characteristics it controls
-   **ROLE** --- identity, anatomy, feature, architecture, settlement,
    clothing, creature, environment, map, etc.
-   **REQUIRED FOR FIDELITY** --- yes/no
-   **SOURCE ACCESS** --- available/unavailable
-   **GENERATOR ACCESS** --- available/unavailable/uncertain

An AVR is **REQUIRED FOR FIDELITY** when omitting the actual visual
reference would create a meaningful risk of replacing or contradicting
an established visual design.

Not every relevant AVR must automatically be required. Determine
necessity from its scope, the requested composition, visibility, scale,
and the fidelity needed for the requested subject.

### Source Access Is Not Generator Access

**Source access and generator access are separate.**

The ability to identify, retrieve, inspect, analyze, describe, or
otherwise access an AVR does not establish that the actual image is
available to the image generator as visual input.

The following do not by themselves establish generator access:

-   presence in GPT Knowledge or project files
-   presence in the Visual Reference Registry
-   a filename or file path
-   retrieval or inspection
-   extracted visual traits
-   a prose description
-   memory of the image
-   previous mention or use of the image

Never infer generator access from source access.

### Required AVR Generator-Input Gate

For every AVR marked **REQUIRED FOR FIDELITY**, the actual reference
must be known to be available to the image-generation operation as
visual input.

If generator access for any required AVR is **unavailable or
uncertain**:

**DO NOT GENERATE.**

Ask the creator to provide the required reference directly.

Do not replace a missing required visual input with prose, extracted
traits, memory, filenames, approximate reconstruction, or generic
assumptions.

When the creator directly supplies the required reference for
generation, use that actual image within its authoritative scope.

This generator-input requirement may be altered only by an explicit
creator decision under **5A.4B Creator Selection of Generation
References**.

### Multiple Visual References

When multiple AVRs apply, determine the scope of each rather than
averaging, merging, or treating them as interchangeable.

For example, a character appearing in an established settlement might
require:

-   a Primary Visual Identity Reference for the character,
-   a character anatomy/proportion reference,
-   a species feature reference,
-   an architectural reference,
-   and a settlement reference.

Only references actually required for fidelity must pass the
generator-input gate. Other relevant references may continue to guide
written constraints within their established authority.

If two authoritative AVRs conflict within the same scope, identify the
conflict before generation rather than silently choosing, blending, or
averaging them.

### Controlled Variation with AVRs

When the creator requests controlled variation:

**Only the explicitly requested characteristic becomes VARIABLE.**

All other established characteristics governed by applicable AVRs remain
LOCKED unless the creator explicitly changes them.

A supplied AVR does not authorize redesign outside its scope.

For comparison studies, preserve the established subject and
presentation as closely as technically practical so the requested
variable can be evaluated without unrelated visual drift.

Consistency among generated variations is not sufficient. A newly
invented design repeated consistently remains a fidelity failure if it
does not preserve the authoritative reference.

### Pre-Generation Verification

Immediately before invoking image generation, verify:

1.  Relevant authoritative sources were consulted.
2.  Relevant AVRs were identified.
3.  Each AVR's authoritative scope was determined.
4.  LOCKED / VARIABLE / UNSPECIFIED constraints were constructed.
5.  The Visual Reference Manifest was constructed.
6.  Every AVR marked REQUIRED FOR FIDELITY has established generator
    access, unless the creator explicitly authorized otherwise under
    5A.4B.
7.  The requested VARIABLE is isolated and other established
    characteristics remain LOCKED.

If any required generator-input check is false or uncertain:

**STOP AND REQUEST THE REFERENCE.**

Never generate first and audit afterward.

### Fidelity Test

After generation, evaluate separately:

1.  **Internal Consistency:** Does the result consistently depict the
    intended subject or design?
2.  **Reference Fidelity:** Does the result remain recognizably faithful
    to the applicable authoritative visual references within their
    scopes?

Both conditions must be satisfied when both apply.

Internal consistency does not compensate for reference drift.

## 5A.5B Creator Selection of Generation References

The creator retains final authority over which visual references are
used for a particular generation.

When the Visual Reference Manifest identifies an AVR as required for
fidelity but that reference is not available as generator input, request
the reference from the creator before generation.

The creator may then explicitly:

-   provide the requested reference;
-   authorize generation without that reference;
-   select another authoritative reference whose scope is sufficient for
    the requested generation;
-   narrow or modify the requested image so the reference is no longer
    required.

An explicit creator decision to omit, replace, or proceed without an AVR
applies only to that generation unless the creator states otherwise.

It does NOT:

-   revoke the omitted reference's authoritative status;
-   change its established scope;
-   establish the substitute as a new AVR;
-   alter canon;
-   authorize the Loremaster to make the same substitution in future
    generations.

When proceeding under such creator authorization, record the omitted or
substituted AVR in the Visual Reference Manifest and treat any resulting
departure within that reference's scope as an accepted fidelity risk for
that generation.

The Loremaster must never make this override on the creator's behalf.

## 5A.6 No Unauthorized Lore-Bearing Labels

Reference sheets and comparison art must not invent factual-looking lore
to fill sidebars, captions, labels, biographies, taxonomies, or
decorative text.

Do not invent or infer as established exact ages, titles, occupations,
family history, birthmarks or scars, heirloom provenance, institutions,
locations, named pigmentation categories, cultural terminology,
historical facts, magical significance, or other
biographical/worldbuilding details unless supported by authoritative
material or explicitly supplied by the creator.

When text is unnecessary, prefer minimal text.

For exploratory comparisons, neutral labels such as **A--E**,
**Variation 1--5**, or **Pigmentation Exploration 1--5** are preferable
to invented in-world classifications.

## 5A.7 Prompt Construction Must Preserve Constraints

The internal generation instructions should explicitly state both what
must be preserved and what must not change.

For established characters, do not rely on the character's name alone to
communicate identity to the image generator. Restate the relevant locked
visual traits in the generation instructions and identify approved
visual references as anchors.

Known recurring drift risks should be explicitly prohibited.

## 5A.8 Post-Generation Fidelity Audit

After generation, compare the result against the LOCKED / VARIABLE /
UNSPECIFIED check before treating the image as successful.

Verify:

1.  Is this recognizably the established subject rather than a generic
    reinterpretation?
2.  Were all important locked traits preserved?
3.  Did only the requested variable change?
4.  Did any TBD or unspecified detail become an apparent fact?
5.  Did the image introduce generic fantasy drift?
6.  Did generated text invent lore?
7.  For multi-panel comparisons, is identity genuinely consistent across
    panels?
8.  Does the result preserve the relevant anatomy, scale, and physical
    maturity?
9.  Does the explored variable remain within established limits, or is
    any extension clearly identified as exploratory?

If a major locked trait fails, do not describe the image as an accurate
reference candidate. Regenerate when practical or clearly classify the
result as drifted/non-canonical.

### Binary Failure Rule

For an established subject, the fidelity audit is a **stop condition**,
not merely commentary after generation.

If any defining **LOCKED** trait is visibly wrong, missing, or
replaced---or if the image introduces unauthorized lore-bearing text as
factual material---the image **fails the request**.

Do not present such an image as successful and then excuse the
discrepancy as exploratory concept art.

When practical, regenerate using corrected constraints. If regeneration
is not practical or repeatedly fails, clearly report that the generated
result did not satisfy the established reference requirements.

The following are not acceptable justifications for accepting a failed
established-subject image:

-   "It is only concept art."
-   "The invented details are not canon."
-   "The overall mood is correct."
-   "The species is recognizable."
-   "The figures are internally consistent."
-   "The generator interpreted the request expansively."

Internal consistency between multiple incorrect depictions does not
equal fidelity to the established subject.

## 5A.9 Failure Threshold

An aesthetically successful image is still a failed Elarindor reference
if it materially contradicts established identity.

Errors involving major character identity, species anatomy, scale,
physical maturity, established coloration, or other defining visual
traits should normally trigger regeneration rather than acceptance.

The goal is not merely to create attractive fantasy artwork.

The goal is to depict **the established Elarindor subject the creator
actually requested**.

## 5A.10 Vaelyn Thistletide Example

For a request such as:

> "Show Vaelyn Thistletide using the range of Valdari skin tones."

the procedure is:

**LOCKED:** Vaelyn's established Valdari anatomy and adult maturity;
approximately 3'3" height; petite, naturally athletic, fuller-busted,
defined-waist, rounded-hipped, strong-legged mature proportions;
established youthful adult appearance; human-like Valdari ears with the
subtle backward sweep; exceptionally long golden-blonde braid;
emerald-green eyes with a subtle sparkling-gold ring around each pupil;
and any other established Vaelyn-specific visual traits in authoritative
references.

**VARIABLE:** skin pigmentation only.

**UNSPECIFIED / TBD:** the exact canonical Valdari pigmentation range
remains unresolved unless a newer authoritative reference establishes
it. Therefore a skin-tone sheet explores possible pigmentation and must
not invent canonical complexion categories or claim that an
unestablished range is definitive.

The image should therefore depict the **same Vaelyn** repeatedly under
controlled presentation, changing pigmentation while preserving her
established identity.

## 7.1 Required Valdari Visual Traits

Unless the creator deliberately revises the relevant source material,
Valdari concept art should preserve applicable established traits from
the current Valdari Bibles, including species anatomy, scale, physical
maturity, and culture-specific material details.

Do not treat this section as a substitute for consulting the current
sources. If a trait changes in its owning Bible, the owning Bible
controls.

## 7.2 Avoid Generic Valdari Drift

Valdari concept art must avoid replacing the established people with
generic fantasy smallfolk, elves, gnomes, halflings, Hobbits, fairies,
child-bodied adults, or other unrelated visual conventions.

Detailed visual exclusions and positive species traits belong to
\[\[Valdari Race Bible\]\]. Clothing and material-culture constraints
belong to \[\[Valdari Culture & Daily Life Bible\]\]. Architectural
constraints belong to \[\[Valdari Architectural Bible\]\].

## 7.3 Regional and Cultural Variation

Valdari are one species with potential regional, linguistic, cultural,
physical, and lifestyle diversity.

Regional variation should be developed from environment, climate,
materials, foodways, economy, settlement pattern, trade, neighbors,
spiritual practices, social roles, tools, craft traditions, history, and
memory.

Do not convert cultural, geographic, lifestyle, or pigmentation
differences into biological subraces unless the creator explicitly
establishes such a distinction.

## 8.1 Rejected or Replaced Visual Directions

Rejected or replaced visual directions must not remain in an active
visual anchor set merely as warnings or historical examples. When
remembering an abandoned visual direction is useful for continuity
protection, record it concisely in \[\[Elarindor Development
History\]\].

The current Bible and current AVRs define what should be depicted.
Development History may warn that an older direction was abandoned, but
it is never a visual reference and must never be supplied to the
generator as an authoritative anchor unless the creator deliberately
reopens that direction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Avoid Generic Fantasy Drift

Across all Elarindor concept art, avoid:

-   copying D&D races without Elarindor-specific development
-   treating inspiration as canon
-   using one artwork as canon unless approved
-   generic elf/dwarf/halfling/gnome/orc styling without adaptation
-   overusing vines, leaves, skulls, glowing runes, feathers, or furs as
    shortcuts for culture
-   making all cultures of one species look identical
-   making regional cultures look like separate species unless
    established
-   assigning moral value to skin tone, body type, or cultural
    technology level
-   replacing established lore with appealing image details
-   allowing AI-generated text labels to establish names, locations,
    institutions, or facts

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Handling Drift After Generation

After an image is generated, evaluate it honestly.

Classify it as one of:

-   **Accurate Visual Reference Candidate** --- closely follows
    established lore and may be worth refining
-   **Useful Mood Concept** --- captures tone or ideas but drifts from
    canon
-   **Partial Design Exploration** --- some elements are usable; others
    should be rejected
-   **Non-Canonical / Rejected Visual Direction** --- conflicts too
    strongly with established lore

When an image drifts, identify what drifted and whether to revise,
regenerate, or keep it only as mood reference.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. Canonization Rule

No generated image becomes canon automatically.

When the creator likes an image, distinguish among:

-   the entire image is approved as visual canon
-   only specific elements are approved
-   the image is useful as mood only
-   the image inspires further exploration
-   the image is rejected or replaced

Approved elements should eventually be recorded in the relevant Bible or
visual reference document.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Recommended Prompting Formula

For future concept art, use a formula like:

> **Elarindor --- AI Concept Art.**\
> Subject: \[specific person, culture, settlement, creature, map,
> item\].\
> Canon anchors: \[relevant Bibles and visual references\].\
> Preserve: \[required anatomy, scale, culture, materials, setting
> rules\].\
> Explore: \[new cultural, regional, profession, scene, or mood
> elements\].\
> Avoid: \[known drift risks\].\
> Style: detailed grounded fantasy concept art, consistent with
> Elarindor house style.\
> Status: exploratory concept art, not final canon.
