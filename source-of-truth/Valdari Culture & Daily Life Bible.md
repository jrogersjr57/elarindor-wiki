# 1. Purpose of This Bible

The **Valdari Race Bible** answers:

> **Who are the Valdari?**

The **Valdari Architectural Bible** answers:

> **How do the Valdari build?**

This document answers:

> **How do the Valdari live?**

It covers the ordinary practices that make a Valdari community feel like
a functioning society rather than simply a collection of fantasy
characters.

This includes:

-   family and household life,
-   food and agriculture,
-   animal husbandry,
-   trades and crafts,
-   commerce,
-   transportation,
-   community life,
-   clothing and personal goods,
-   customs and traditions,
-   and the rhythms of everyday life.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. Core Cultural Principles

Valdari daily life reflects the broader cultural values established in
the [[Worldbuilding/Elarindor/AI Sources of Truth/Valdari Race Bible]].

These include:

-   **Stewardship**
-   **Community**
-   **Patience**
-   **Observation**
-   **Craftsmanship**
-   **Learning**
-   **Memory**
-   **Tradition**
-   **Curiosity**

The Valdari do not generally view civilization and nature as opposing
forces.

They regard themselves as participants within the environments in which
they live.

This philosophy influences farming, forestry, animal husbandry,
construction, trade, craftwork, and the organization of settlements.

Taking from the land is not inherently considered wrong.

Taking thoughtlessly is.

## 3.2 Children

Children are deeply valued within Valdari society.

Their upbringing involves more than their immediate parents.

Children commonly interact with:

-   parents,
-   siblings,
-   extended family,
-   neighbors,
-   craftspeople,
-   Elders,
-   and other members of the community.

For the biological stages of Valdari infancy, childhood, adolescence,
and adulthood, see [[Worldbuilding/Elarindor/AI Sources of Truth/Valdari Race Bible]].

### Foundational Learning

Valdari childhood education is rooted primarily in the home, family, and
surrounding community rather than in a universal modern-style school
system.

Foundational learning includes the basic knowledge and skills necessary
for the child to function increasingly independently within Valdari
society.

Established foundational subjects include:

-   spoken language,
-   reading,
-   writing,
-   basic mathematics.

Other universal areas of childhood instruction remain to be established.

Learning also occurs continually through:

-   observation,
-   imitation,
-   play,
-   storytelling,
-   helping adults,
-   exploration,
-   and participation in everyday community life.

A child accompanying family members to a market, workshop, garden, farm,
apiary, or neighboring household may therefore be learning while
participating in ordinary life.

Children may begin acquiring familiarity with family occupations and
practical skills long before entering formal professional training.

Growing up around a particular occupation may provide years of practical
familiarity without requiring the young Valdari to choose that
occupation for themselves.

Family occupation does **not** automatically determine adult profession.

## 4.4 Beekeeping

**Status: TBD**

Beekeeping belongs within Valdari agriculture and food production.

Relationship with the bees. The bees are native wild bees, not domesticated livestock. Valdari encourage, protect, house, and manage colonies but have not selectively bred them into a domesticated species. Beekeeping is therefore managed coexistence and stewardship.

Cultural availability. Honey and edible honeycomb are important parts of Valdari daily life and cuisine. However, home apiaries are uncommon. Most Valdari obtain bee products through professional beekeepers and the market. A household that does maintain bees normally has only a small tiered apiary sufficient to supplement family use, particularly cooking honey and edible comb.

Professional production. Most production comes from professional beekeepers operating permanent apiaries. Professional production is specialized principally around liquid honey, edible comb, or wax, rather than expecting every professional apiary to maximize all three.

Hive forms. Horizontal, stacked/tiered, and semi-fixed/rear-access arrangements can all exist. There is no universal exterior hive shape. Selection depends on site, purpose, colony needs, and keeper practice.

Hive organization. The brood/core and surplus areas are functionally distinct. The brood core prioritizes colony stability and is rarely disturbed. It does not use the removable peg harvesting system. Surplus comb is deliberately accessible for harvesting.

The division between brood and surplus relies upon spacing, entrance placement, and chamber geometry, not a mechanical queen excluder. The bees’ primary entrance leads naturally toward the brood/core rather than directly into the harvest area. Beekeeper access is positioned away from the principal flight path.

Surplus-comb support. The harvestable surplus area uses shaped wooden supports and structural pegs that bees incorporate into the comb. The pegs are not pulled through completed comb. External retaining joinery releases the support, pegs, and comb together as one assembly. This is deliberately not a modern rectangular frame.

Permanent and temporary apiaries. Permanent professional apiaries use permanent Valdari construction principles, including a low, structurally continuous stone foundation, rather than isolated/gapped stone piers. Temporary or seasonal apiaries may instead use compact peg-joined timber platforms without permanent masonry.

Observation and defensive behavior. Keepers observe the colony before opening it: flight activity, guards, pollen activity, agitation, weather, and other indicators. Controlled cool smoke is used sparingly, primarily to reduce defensive escalation rather than overwhelm the colony.

If soldiers settle, work may proceed. If warning behavior intensifies, the normal response is to stop and return another time unless colony health requires intervention.

Harvesting. Harvest is taken from mature surplus comb rather than the brood core. The keeper judges colony reserves, brood encroachment, pollen stores, colony strength, forage/weather conditions, disease or damage, and behavior before deciding whether anything can safely be taken. Opening a hive does not guarantee a harvest, and there is no universal percentage that must be removed.

Whole sections of mature surplus comb are removed, but in sizes manageable by a Valdari keeper.

Because a sting is fatal to the bee and venom is potent, causing soldiers to sting during routine work represents poor handling. Skilled beekeeping emphasizes accomplishing necessary work with as little defensive escalation as possible.

Bee clearing. Bees are first given an opportunity to leave the removed comb voluntarily. Orientation toward the hive and very restrained smoke can assist. The accepted wafting paddle produces gentle directional airflow to encourage remaining bees to move without striking or brushing them. A conventional bee brush is not the primary method.

Protective equipment. The accepted basic clothing is a structured-face hood, a tightly woven smooth-surfaced overshirt, and flexible leather work gloves. Protection comes primarily from coverage, smooth surfaces, spacing between bee and skin, and controlled fit rather than bulky armor-like clothing.

Structured-face hood. The protective hood encloses the entire head, including the Valdari ears. Its structure provides sufficient lateral and rearward clearance for the ears to retain their natural swept-back position without pressing against the fabric or mesh. No part of the ear protrudes through the protective enclosure. The face and ears remain separated from the outer mesh or fabric so that a bee landing on the hood cannot readily sting through to the skin

Comb handling. Large harvested comb is supported in a shallow rigid comb cradle rather than carried dangling from its wooden support. Exact liner material and some construction details remain TBD.

Honey processing. The Valdari use a combined method. Good edible comb can remain intact for eating. Other harvested comb is opened and allowed to drain for liquid honey, followed where appropriate by gentle pressing.

The accepted press direction is a wooden screw press, mechanically comparable to a book press: a timber frame and wooden screw gradually lower a pressure surface onto drained comb. Pressure is applied progressively rather than violently.

The balance between edible comb, liquid honey, and recovered wax is need-dependent rather than governed by a fixed percentage. When wax is plentiful, more suitable comb can be retained for eating while honey is collected from the remainder. When wax is needed, more harvested comb can enter processing and wax recovery.

Harvest containers. The accepted direction is mixed: rigid wooden boxes protect delicate mature comb, while lighter woven containers can carry tools, wax, partial comb, and less-fragile material. Several modest containers are preferable to one enormous load.

#### The Beekeepers’ Guild
There is one overarching Beekeepers’ Guild. Within it are three specialized schools, currently functionally identified as the Honey School, Comb School, and Wax School. Those names can remain working terminology until we decide whether they need distinct Valdari names.

The guild controls and oversees the network of large permanent professional apiaries. These are not all concentrated into one communal apiary district; permanent apiaries can be located in different suitable areas.

Each qualified keeper is entrusted with responsibility for one or two permanent apiary structures. The keeper manages those colonies and is permitted to sell the product from their harvest.

Guild membership requires dues/tribute, payable in coinage or product. Exact amounts, frequency, and financial structure remain TBD.

The guild maintains awareness of the status of all apiaries and colonies under its authority. Keepers therefore report meaningful changes, problems, unusual behavior, disease concerns, production conditions, and other relevant observations. A problem encountered at one apiary can become a lesson for the entire guild.

Beekeeper apprenticeship

A Valdari who wants professional beekeeping as their path goes to the Beekeepers’ Guild and requests an apprenticeship. The guild assigns the applicant to an available qualified master keeper rather than requiring the applicant to independently find one.

The apprentice initially learns under that keeper and within that keeper’s school.

During apprenticeship, the apprentice spends a season or two training with the other schools, gaining experience with the other major sides of the profession. Most ultimately remain with their original school, but some discover through this training that another specialization suits them better and transfer.

Completion is based upon demonstrated competence rather than simply elapsed time or reaching biological adulthood. This preserves the existing Culture Bible’s distinction between professional competence and the Valdari coming-of-age milestone. 

Before independent placement, the apprentice must successfully demonstrate their knowledge and judgment before the guild elders.

The guild does not deliberately stress a healthy hive to create an examination. Evaluation can draw upon the apprentice’s accumulated performance, normal work with healthy colonies, questioning/problem-solving, previous guild cases, and naturally occurring colony situations. When a genuine problem occurs somewhere in the guild network, it may become a teaching opportunity or—when appropriate—a graduation demonstration.

If qualification is successful, the guild either assigns the new keeper to an available permanent apiary or establishes a new permanent structure where expansion is appropriate..

The biology of any Bramble-native bee species belongs in [[BrambleFauna Bible](#bramble-fauna-bible)] rather than here.

## 6.8 Specialized Education & Apprenticeship

Professional and specialized education is distinct from foundational
childhood education.

Depending upon the discipline, advanced training may eventually involve:

-   apprenticeships,
-   guild instruction,
-   individual masters or mentors,
-   magical teachers or traditions,
-   practical occupational training,
-   scholarly study,
-   or other systems still to be developed.

Different professions may require substantially different lengths of
training.

A profession requiring approximately a decade of formal training and one
requiring several decades of training can therefore coexist within the
same Valdari developmental system.

Completion of professional training is **not** a prerequisite for
biological adulthood, nor does completing training early automatically
make a Valdari an adult.

A pre-adult Valdari may become professionally competent, perform serious
work, and carry meaningful community responsibilities before reaching
full adulthood at approximately 50.

Likewise, particularly demanding disciplines may continue beyond
adulthood.

Exact apprenticeship ages, guild structures, professional
qualifications, and training durations remain **TBD**.

Concept — Guilds as intergenerational repositories of specialized knowledge and professional tradition.

**Status: Working Canon**

Valdari reach full biological and recognized social adulthood at
approximately **50 years of age**, as established in [[Valdari Race
Bible]].

Valdari culture recognizes this transition through some form of
coming-of-age or adulthood-recognition ceremony.

The exact:

-   name of the ceremony,
-   ritual form,
-   participants,
-   community role,
-   relationship to family,
-   relationship to profession or apprenticeship,
-   rights or responsibilities conferred,
-   and regional variation

remain **TBD**.

Coming of age should not automatically be treated as:

-   graduation from a profession,
-   completion of an apprenticeship,
-   the first time an individual performs adult-level work,
-   or an automatic requirement to leave the family home.

Professional competence, household residence, employment, and biological
adulthood are related aspects of Valdari life but are not identical
milestones.

A Valdari approaching adulthood may already be carrying substantial
professional or community responsibilities while still residing within
the family household.

# 13. Relationship to Bramble's Ecology

The Valdari are native to [[Bramble]], but Bramble's plants and
animals should not automatically be classified as Valdari.

This distinction is important.

A species may have a particularly close relationship with the Valdari
while still being part of the wider ecology of Bramble.

Detailed biological information about native species should therefore
eventually belong in a separate:

> **\[[Bramble Fauna Bible](#bramble-fauna-bible)\]**

The division should generally be:

### Bramble Fauna Bible

Answers:

> **What is this creature?**

Including:

-   anatomy,
-   habitat,
-   diet,
-   behavior,
-   reproduction,
-   ecology,
-   natural range,
-   predators,
-   wild populations,
-   and evolutionary or magical history.

-   breeding,
-   agriculture,

-   companionship,
-   animal products,
-   cultural significance,
-   care,

# 17. Education --- Development Notes

The following principles are currently established for future
development of Valdari education:

-   foundational childhood learning is primarily rooted in home, family,
    and community;
-   spoken language, reading, writing, and basic mathematics are
    established foundational subjects;
-   observation, imitation, play, storytelling, helping adults,
    exploration, and participation in daily life are important learning
    methods;
-   practical exposure to a family occupation does not determine a young
    Valdari's eventual profession;
-   specialized professional education is distinct from foundational
    childhood learning;
-   different professions may require substantially different training
    durations;
-   a pre-adult Valdari may become professionally competent and perform
    serious work before age 50;
-   professional qualification does not itself confer adulthood;
-   reaching adulthood does not require professional training to be
    complete;
-   and Valdari learning may continue throughout adult life.

Exact apprenticeship ages, guild structures, curricula, professional
qualifications, and training durations remain **TBD**.
